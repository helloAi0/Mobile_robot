# Mechanical model, physics and sensors

## Package layout
| package | contents |
|---|---|
| `mobile_robot_description` | `urdf/` xacro (chassis, sprung drive carriers, swivel caster, sensors, ros2_control), `meshes/` (visual STL), `tools/` (generators), `launch/rsp.launch.py`, `view_robot.launch.py` |
| `mobile_robot_gazebo` | `worlds/` (house, street, test worlds), `tools/` (world generators), `launch/gazebo.launch.py` (Gazebo + spawn + bridges + controllers), `scripts/scan_degrader.py` |
| `mobile_robot_bringup` | `launch/` (sim, hardware, autonomy), `config/` (controllers, SLAM), `scripts/` (mux, autopilot, teleop, metrics), `rviz/`, tests |

## Mass and inertia
`mobile_robot_description/tools/gen_mass_properties.py` holds a component table (chassis plate, battery, computer, motors, wheels,
caster, lidar, camera, IMU). It builds each body's mass, centre of mass and full inertia tensor with the parallel-axis theorem and
writes `urdf/mass_properties.xacro`. Edit the table and re-run it, or replace the numbers with your real CAD export
(Onshape/Fusion/SolidWorks mass-properties output). **The values are computed from primitive shapes with the dimensions in the
table; they are not exported from a CAD file, because none exists in this repo.** Tests check every tensor is positive definite and
obeys the triangle inequality, and that the masses sum to the budget.

## Visual vs. collision
Visuals are STL meshes (`meshes/`, generated by `tools/gen_meshes.py`). Collisions are a few boxes and cylinders only; a test fails if
a collision uses a mesh. To use your own CAD/Blender export, save it under the same file name (`chassis.stl` or `.dae`).

## Drivetrain and suspension
- Each drive wheel hangs on a prismatic spring/damper carrier (k = 1450 N/m, damping ratio 0.6) with travel stops.
- The caster is a vertical swivel bearing + 25 mm trail + a rolling wheel (continuous joints, small friction and damping), not a sphere.
- Drive tyre mu 1.0 / 0.9, caster mu 0.6; floor mu 0.9 with slip 0.002 and torsional friction; the house has a wet-tile patch
  (mu 0.15) and a rubber mat to make odometry slip visibly.
- Actuator limits: 2 N*m and 12 rad/s per wheel in the URDF; `my_controllers.yaml` also clamps velocity (0.6 m/s, 2 rad/s) and
  acceleration. **Backlash is not modelled** (Gazebo has no gearbox-backlash primitive).

## Engine
`house_world.sdf` uses DART at 2 ms steps (the best-supported articulated-body engine in Gazebo Harmonic). Friction, slip and
torsional values are in the floor and patch surfaces; `kp`/`kd` are set on the wheels but only the ODE engine reads them.

## Sensors
| sensor | model |
|---|---|
| 2D lidar (RPLidar A1 class) | 720 samples, 10 Hz, 12 m, Gaussian range noise; `scan_degrader.py` adds dropouts and a slow range-bias walk; a mask hides the beams blocked by the mounting pillar |
| OAK-D class RGB-D camera | 640x400, 15 Hz, 72 deg HFOV, barrel distortion, image noise |
| IMU (BMI270 class) | 100 Hz, Gaussian noise, constant turn-on bias and a slowly drifting bias, from datasheet values |

## Not verified here
This was built and unit-tested without a Gazebo or ROS runtime. The suspension, caster behaviour, door hinges, friction values and
the SDF physics settings must be checked on your machine: drive over the ramp and the wet patch, push a door, and compare
`/imu` and `/diff_cont/odom` against the commanded motion.
